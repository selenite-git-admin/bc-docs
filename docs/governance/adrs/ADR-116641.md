# A profile is a parent manifest plus typed children — composition, not one file

## Context

Measured on 2026-08-06. pilots/profiles/mfg-in/master.json is 175,845 bytes across 7,539 lines. Of that, 135,434 bytes (77%) is machine-generated registry — 208 customers, 112 vendors, 50 items, 40 raw materials, 6 sub-assemblies — produced deterministically by master_gen.py, whose own docstring states "same seed => byte-identical registry". Another 7,013 bytes (4%) is hand-authored benchmark policy. They share one file and therefore one hash.

The consequence was felt repeatedly this week. Every band change — gross margin 42-66 to 35-55, adding operations_structure — re-hashed 208 generated customers. Conversely, regenerating the registry from a new seed would silently invalidate the identity of authored policy nobody had touched. Neither artefact could be cited independently, which matters now that world builds cite profile identity in their closures.

The same file also existed twice. data/profiles/mfg-in/master.json and pilots/profiles/mfg-in/master.json had diverged on six of seven keys — customers 55K versus 75K, benchmarks 1.7K versus 7.0K, and raw materials, sub-assemblies and scale entirely absent from the older copy. Nothing read the stale one; its path survived only in docstrings, and two neighbouring files already carried comments recording that this exact trap had bitten in July and again on 2026-08-04. Monolithic files invite copies, and copies drift silently because no part of them is independently identified.

The parent-child shape was the operator's proposal, by analogy with CloudFormation nested stacks, and it is better than the flat split first considered because it declares the relationships rather than only separating the content. The three jobs a parent template does — manifest, parameter passing, dependency order — all map onto real properties of a profile that are currently implicit in generator code.

Point 6 exists because a 7,539-line file becoming seven files cannot be meaningfully reviewed by diff. The audit has to be a proof of property, not an inspection of change. Capturing the pre-split canonical hash first is called out explicitly because computing a baseline after the fact is a failure mode this programme has produced more than once: a check whose inputs derive from its own outputs cannot fail.

Point 4 is a boundary against a specific drift. The operator's phrasing was that the parent should carry steps and sequences; on reflection the durable part is dependency, not procedure. Procedure already has a home in the build runbook, and duplicating it into the profile would create two authorities for the same question.

Point 7 is stated as a checkable property because portability is decided but unproven: DEC-731c15 and D524 commit to Business Central alongside Odoo, and DEC-def930 makes Odoo the base world with BC and Salesforce as projections. A profile that quietly accumulates Odoo account codes would fail that at the worst moment. A related finding recorded during this analysis and not resolved here: sim/dynamics.py imports its decision functions from the Odoo generator, so the control-loop model is coupled to one system's implementation and would need a neutral home before a second generator exists.

Supporting measurement is in the session record for SES-3c0b11. Related: DEC-def930 (extended), DEC-d308de (profile #1 scale), DEC-b0839a (one core, many projections), DEC-e1241a (derive versus preserve), DEC-731c15 and D524 (BC and Odoo).

## Decision

A pilot profile is composed, not monolithic: a parent manifest plus typed children. This EXTENDS DEC-def930 (Profile x Projection x Time; source-agnostic profile; committed master-entity registry with cross-system IDs) by specifying the internal composition that ADR left open. It supersedes nothing.

1. THE PARENT IS A MANIFEST, NOT A CONTAINER. It declares profile identity and archetype, shared parameters, the child list with each child's kind and sha256, and the generation dependency order. It does not contain child content. A reader who opens the parent learns what the profile is and what it is made of without loading 175KB.

2. EVERY CHILD DECLARES ITS KIND — authored or generated. Authored children are policy written by a human: their hash is lineage and changing one is a governed act. Generated children are derivable from a declared seed and generator: their hash proves reproduction and they may be regenerated without review. This is DEC-e1241a's derive-versus-preserve distinction applied one level down, for the same reason — the two want different treatment and must not share an identity.

3. SHARED PARAMETERS ARE DECLARED ONCE, IN THE PARENT, AND FLOW DOWN. Seed, window and scale are inputs to several generated children. They are parameters of the profile, not properties of any child, and repeating them invites drift.

4. THE PARENT CARRIES A DEPENDENCY GRAPH, NOT A PROCEDURE. Generation order is real and must be declared: raw materials before sub-assemblies before items, because bills reference them. But a profile that carries steps becomes a second runbook, and two documents claiming to say how things happen will drift. The profile declares what depends on what; the runbook declares what to run when.

5. SPLIT BY CHANGE RATE, OWNER AND DERIVATION KIND — never by entity type for its own sake. That lands at roughly six children for mfg-in: identity, scale, benchmarks, customers, vendors, catalogue (items plus materials plus sub-assemblies), anomalies. Splitting 208 customers into per-customer files would be fragmentation without benefit.

6. TWO CONSERVATION PROOFS ARE MANDATORY, at the split and at every build thereafter. CONSERVATION: an assemble step reconstitutes the flat shape and its canonicalised hash must equal the canonicalised hash of the pre-split original — captured BEFORE the split, or it proves nothing. DERIVATION: regenerating every generated child from its declared seed must reproduce the committed bytes. MANIFEST INTEGRITY: declared child hashes must equal actual. All three are runbook gates that HALT, not one-time checks.

7. A PROFILE REMAINS SOURCE-AGNOSTIC, restating DEC-def930's boundary as a checkable property of every child: no GL account codes, no ERP model or table names, no journal names, no system-specific identifiers. Cross-system join keys (CUST-, VEND-, ITEM-) are the declared exception and are not platform identity.

8. A PROFILE DECLARES A JURISDICTION RATHER THAN EMBEDDING ONE. Statutory facts — GST rates and
state codes, professional-tax slabs, PF and ESI thresholds and rates, TDS tables, the fiscal
calendar, Schedule II depreciation lives — are properties of a country, not of a business model.
They are identical for a manufacturer, a services firm and a retailer in the same jurisdiction,
and they differ for the same business model across jurisdictions. They live in a shared
jurisdiction child that a profile references by id and version. Industry policy (benchmark bands)
and jurisdiction policy (statutory rates) are orthogonal axes and are therefore separate children,
not one policy blob.

## Consequences

- A band change no longer re-hashes 208 generated customers. Authored policy gains an identity citable in a build closure independently of the registry.
- Generated children become honestly disposable: regenerate, compare, discard. Only the seed and the generator need preserving.
- **Registry children are never shared between profiles.** This was claimed in an earlier draft and is wrong. A manufacturer's customers are OEM buyers on 45-90 day terms; a retailer's are distributors and counter sales; a services firm's are retainer clients. Nothing transfers, and the composition must not imply that it does. Sharing across profiles happens only at the jurisdiction layer, where the shared content is statutory rather than commercial.
- The jurisdiction split has an immediate consumer. Three expense classes absent from the mfg-in world — income tax, PF and ESI, depreciation — are all statutory. Encoding them inside one profile guarantees the next profile re-derives Indian payroll tax independently and differently.
- Three new HALT gates enter the build runbook (conservation, derivation, manifest integrity). They run every build, not once at the split.
- Every existing reader of `master.json` must either move to the children or call the assemble step. The assemble step exists partly so that migration need not be simultaneous.

## Consequences not claimed

Composition does not make profiles cheaper to author. A second profile still needs its own registry, its own bands, and its own generator parameters. What it buys is that a change to one concern stops invalidating the identity of the others, and that statutory facts are authored once per jurisdiction instead of once per profile.

## Open items

- **`sim/dynamics.py` couples the control-loop model to the Odoo generator** by importing its decision functions from `seed.py`. Business logic in a system-specific file; needs a neutral home before a second generator exists. Not resolved here.
- **Child granularity is proposed, not fixed.** Roughly six children plus a jurisdiction reference for mfg-in. The boundary between a single `catalogue` child and separate `items` / `raw_materials` / `sub_assemblies` children should settle after the split is executed once.
- ~~**Jurisdiction child scope is undecided.**~~ **RESOLVED 2026-08-06 — see Amendment 1.**
- **Naming form undecided.** Directory plus short names (`mfg-in/customers.json`) is less redundant; dotted flat files are self-identifying if moved.
- **Profile renaming was considered and rejected.** `mfg-in` is a handle, not a description; `profile.json` already carries `display_name`, `archetype` and `profile_number`. Renaming would touch 63 references to buy information one file away.

## Status note

`proposed`, not `decided`. Nothing is split yet. Execution rides the Odoo Enterprise greenfield rebuild, where a new profile directory is created anyway and no sweep against a live tree is needed. The pre-split canonical hash must be captured before any file is touched.

## Amendment 1 — jurisdiction child scope, and effective dating (2026-08-06)

Operator decision, same day: build the jurisdiction child, **statutory rates only**. This closes
the open item on scope and adds one material qualification to point 8 that the original decision
did not carry.

### A1.1 Scope is rates only

The child carries statutory parameters and nothing else. It does **not** carry a chart-of-accounts
template, `l10n_in` module expectations, or any other system-aware content. Either would make the
child Odoo-aware and breach point 7, and a profile that quietly knows about one ERP fails the
portability commitment in DEC-731c15 and D524 at the worst possible moment.

"Rates only" is not self-defining, so the line is drawn explicitly: the child carries **rates,
ceilings, thresholds, slab boundaries, due dates and useful lives**; the **computation stays in
the generator**. `in.json` states that provident fund is 12% of a wage capped at ₹15,000; it never
states how to compute the deduction, because that is where regime choices and exceptions live.
s.192 is a slab-plus-surcharge-plus-cess computation, advance tax is a schedule, and Schedule II is
lives plus a method choice — all three are computations that consume parameters rather than being
parameters. Enforced by law J4.

### A1.2 Parameters are effective-dated — a correction to point 8 as written

Point 8 described a jurisdiction child without saying that its contents vary over time. A flat rate
table is wrong for any world spanning more than one fiscal year, and this is not a theoretical
concern. Inside mfg-in's own 2021-04-01 → 2026-07-31 window:

| date | change |
|---|---|
| 2021-07-01 | s.194Q TDS on purchase of goods commences (one quarter into the window) |
| 2023-04-01 | Maharashtra professional tax female exemption threshold 10,000 → 25,000 |
| 2023-04-01 | Karnataka professional tax threshold 15,000 → 25,000 |
| 2023-04-01 | new-regime individual slabs revised |
| 2024-04-01 | new-regime individual slabs revised |
| 2025-04-01 | s.206C(1H) TCS on sale of goods omitted |
| 2025-04-01 | new-regime individual slabs revised |
| 2025-09-22 | GST slab rationalisation |

Seven of seven sampled parameters resolve differently at 2021-05 than at 2026-06. So every
parameter is a list of `{effective_from, effective_to, value}` and **resolution is by transaction
date, never by "current"** — implicit head-state selection is precisely what Invariant IV names
inadmissible, and it is the same reasoning that forbids re-deriving a source catalog at bootstrap
in DEC-e1241a.

A version whose value is `null` means **in force as absent** (194Q before July 2021) and is
distinct from an uncovered date, which is a hole in the file and halts.

This defect class is invisible to structural laws: a world using 2026 rates throughout is
internally consistent and legally anachronistic. It is the same class as the B2C-shaped ad spend
and the all-Won CRM in the dry run, both of which were operator-caught and not gate-caught.

### A1.3 A shared file buys review, not accuracy

The claim that a jurisdiction child improves accuracy does not survive scrutiny and is not made
here. If a rate is authored wrong, sharing it makes it uniformly wrong in every profile that reads
it. What the split actually buys is a **single point of review and a single point of fix** — a
precondition for accuracy, not the thing itself.

Accuracy has to come from provenance, so every version carries a citation naming the statute,
section or notification, and an `as_of` date. All 46 versions in the first `in.json` are marked
`unverified`; none has been checked against a primary source. The entries nearest the authoring
model's knowledge cutoff — the 2025-09 GST rationalisation and the FY26 slabs — are the ones a
reviewer should check first. Enforced by law J3: a parameter without a unit, a citation and an
`as_of` is a number nobody can audit.

### A1.4 The envelope is shared; parameter names are jurisdiction-local

`jurisdictions/` holds one file per jurisdiction — `in.json`, and `eu.json` / `us.json` as
siblings when profiles need them. What is common across them is the **envelope**: effective-dated
versions, provenance, units, and the six laws. The **key sets are not common** — `provident_fund.*`
has no US or EU analogue, and `fica.*` has no Indian one. A generator written against `in` asks for
names that do not exist in `us`. That is a real portability limit and is recorded rather than
hidden. Law J-generic proves the laws are envelope-generic by running them against a synthetic
jurisdiction sharing no keys with `in.json`.

Jurisdiction binds at the **legal entity**, not only at the profile, because a group can span
jurisdictions. mfg-in's three entities are all Indian but sit in three states, which is the same
problem one level down: professional tax is a state levy, so each entity declares a
`tax_subdivision` and law J6 halts if that subdivision has no parameters.

### A1.5 What was built

- `bc-sdg/pilots/jurisdictions/in.json` — 35 parameters, 46 versions, all `unverified`
- `bc-sdg/pilots/jurisdictions/jurisdiction.py` — resolver plus laws J1 chronology, J2 window
  coverage, J3 provenance, J4 parameters-not-computation, J5 source-agnostic, J6 profile binding
- `bc-sdg/pilots/profiles/mfg-in/profile.json` — declares the jurisdiction and pins
  `schema_version`; each legal entity declares `tax_subdivision`

All six laws are proven to fire: `--self-test` breaks the world eighteen ways and requires each
injection to halt, with a clean control that must pass. A gate never proven to go red is a green
light with unknown wiring.

**Still `proposed`.** Points 1–7 — the parent/child split itself — remain unexecuted and ride the
Enterprise greenfield rebuild. Point 8 is now built and gated ahead of them, because the statutory
parameters are needed for the three missing expense classes (income tax, PF/ESI, depreciation) that
currently hold the `benchmarks` gate red.
