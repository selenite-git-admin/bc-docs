# The Chief's deputy: the Architect stands in on operator-facing duties when the Chief is silent; scope, limits and records

## Context

ADR DEC-41e4bc routes the fleet through one session. Point 2: escalation runs worker, its controller, the area's owner, the Chief, the operator (authority only), and an unmapped area goes to the Chief. Point 5: the operator talks to the Chief and the controllers. Point 10: the Chief runs the start-of-day and the wind-down. No document says what happens when the Chief's session is silent.

It happened twice. On 2026-10-01 the Chief ended a turn while owing the baseline page, and nothing started a new turn for 1 h 30 min. The night before, the Chief's session timers left no trace for five hours. A Claude session acts only when something starts a turn: a message, a finished background task, or a session timer; timers die with an app restart. The operator's words, recorded in TSK-53fc10: "If a Chief sleeps, the project is down already."

The Chief liveness design (barecount-devhub PR 173, `artifacts/controllers/DESIGN-chief-liveness-2026-10-01.md`) answers with two paths. The primary path is outside every session: the launchd watchdog reads DevHub once a minute and alerts the operator on Slack. The secondary path is a deputy. Codex accepted revision 2 of that design as the build basis (gen-e2cae5-01, ACCEPTED WITH BOUNDARY, design head 7704f7e9), with the boundary that the deputy appointment is the operator's decision and that the Architect then records it in an ADR and in both charters. Revision 3 (head e1c0d527) carries the operator's choice of the deputy's tick: option A, a small watcher session owned by the Architect, every 30 minutes, around the clock. That choice is desk request `2026-10-01T04-05-37-203Z-5e877045`, pending when this record was drafted; it is authority only once granted.

This is an ADR and not an erratum. ADR-ERR-004 corrected a stated fact in DEC-41e4bc (the roster count) on the operator's direction. A controller that may act in the Chief's place is new decision content: a role, its limits and its records. It amends the escalation model and leaves DEC-41e4bc's ten points in force.

The Architect is the deputy for three reasons the documents give. It already holds the review duty over the Chief's deliverables (the fleet design, barecount-devhub PR 148, section 6.3: "The Chief's own deliverables need the Architect and the operator"). The controller chapter's provisional human role map places Chief, Architect and Audit together as the operator's authority roles. And the model policy keeps the Chief and the Architect on the same model, so the stand-in reads the Chief's material at the same depth.

## Decision

1. **The deputy pair.** The Architect is the Chief's deputy. The Chief is the Architect's deputy. No other controller has a deputy under this record; the pattern may be extended by a later decision.

2. **When a deputy stands in.** Only when both hold: the liveness check shows the principal's live session silent beyond the decided threshold (20 minutes in the liveness design), and a wake message sent to the principal went unanswered through the deputy's next tick. Or when the operator says so. A stand-in ends when the principal's session shows activity again, or when the operator says so. A deputy never stands in because it disagrees with the principal or finds the principal slow.

3. **What the Chief's deputy does while standing in: relay and visibility.**
   - It reads the Chief's inbox and the desk's open questions.
   - It tells the operator what is waiting and who owns it.
   - It relays alerts.
   - It repeats the wake at each tick.
   - It answers a controller's "who owns this" from the responsibility map as recorded, without changing the map.

4. **What it never does.** It does not rule on ownership or routing, or decide a transfer that escalated to the Chief. It does not approve a brief. It does not build or seal the attested-review list. It does not open or close the day. It does not decide anything the operator gave the Chief. It does not act on live services. Those wait for the Chief or go to the operator.

5. **No self-review.** What a deputy produces while standing in is reviewed by the operator, or by the principal on return, never by the deputy. While standing in, the Architect gives no Foundation-fit verdict on anything it handled as deputy.

6. **The record.** Each stand-in period is recorded as data, not left in a conversation: one task tagged `stand-in`, owned by the deputy's controller, opened at the start with the evidence (silent since, wake sent at), listing each act, and closed at handback with a summary. The principal acknowledges the handback. Every desk request, Slack line or inbox line made while standing in says so: "Architect, standing in for the Chief".

7. **The watcher.** The deputy's check runs in a small watcher session owned by the deputy's controller, in the worker role. On each tick it makes one DevHub read and ends in one line when nothing is wrong. It may send two kinds of session message: a wake to the principal, and a call to the deputy. It writes nothing else and decides nothing. The tick interval is the operator's recorded choice (option A: 30 minutes, around the clock; desk request `2026-10-01T04-05-37-203Z-5e877045` once granted). Its specification is a descriptive document in barecount-devhub; this record governs it.

8. **Primary path unchanged.** The alert from the watchdog to the operator on Slack is the primary path and depends on no session. The deputy is the secondary path. This record does not authorise the watchdog probe, the session-start reminder hook, the database page or the webhook; each keeps its own approval.

9. **Before a stand-in counts as coverage,** all five hold:
   1. this record is decided, and the charter text below is in the chapter;
   2. the operator has given the deputy a way to post to the operator's Slack channel through a script that never exposes the webhook;
   3. the liveness read exists in DevHub;
   4. the tick is decided and the session-start reminder to re-create timers is installed;
   5. the stand-in record of item 6 is in use.

   Until then the deputy checks the principal at its own ticks as best effort, and no document describes that as coverage.

10. **The charters.** When this record is decided, the text under "Charter text" is added to `docs/development/the-controller-operating-model.md` in the same commit that flips this record's status. Briefs restate the charter; they do not extend it.

## Charter text

To the section "The Escalation Ladder", one sentence at the end of its first paragraph:

> When the Chief's session is silent, the Architect stands in on the Chief's operator-facing duties under DEC-6f7615. The ladder itself does not change, and nothing the Chief decides is decided by the deputy.

To the Chief charter, a new paragraph after "Relationships":

> **Deputy.** The Architect is the Chief's deputy (DEC-6f7615). When the liveness check shows the Chief's session silent and a wake has gone unanswered through one tick, the Architect stands in: it reads the Chief's inbox and the desk's open questions, tells the operator what is waiting and who owns it, relays alerts and repeats the wake. It never rules on ownership or routing, approves a brief, builds or seals the attested-review list, opens or closes the day, or decides anything the operator gave the Chief. Each stand-in period is recorded and handed back with a summary the Chief acknowledges. The Chief is, in turn, the Architect's deputy on the same terms.

To the Architect charter, a new paragraph after "Relationships":

> **Deputy for the Chief.** The Architect stands in for the Chief on operator-facing duties when the Chief's session is silent (DEC-6f7615), through a small watcher session it owns that checks the Chief on the decided tick. Standing in is relay and visibility, never decision. What the Architect produces while standing in is reviewed by the operator or by the Chief on return, and the Architect gives no Foundation-fit verdict on anything it handled as deputy. When the Architect's own session is silent, the Chief stands in on the same terms: Foundation-fit reviews and override adjudications wait, and the Chief tells the operator.

## Foundation gate

- **Repair location:** B, the governance grammar. The escalation model declares no rule for a silent Chief; that is a missing declaration.
- **Why not an upper layer:** Foundation does not govern the fleet. The ADR layer is where DEC-41e4bc lives.
- **Why not a lower layer:** the watchdog probe is a detector. Under DEC-c48b0f item 5 a detector is a net, not a fix; the design act, who acts on the alert and within what limits, is named first.
- **Design act.** No platform invariant is touched.

## Rationale

The fleet design's own principles decide the shape. "Enforce in the system, never in memory": the stand-in is a recorded period with a task, not a habit. "Detect; don't trust self-report": the trigger is the liveness check, not the deputy's opinion. "Every gate can be overridden, visibly": a stand-in is visible in the Chief's inbox, on the desk and in DevHub. The limits in item 4 follow from where the Chief's authority comes from: the operator gave those decisions to the Chief, and a deputy that took them would be a second Chief without the operator's word. The no-self-review rule keeps the one independent check on the Chief's work independent.

## Options considered

- **No deputy; Slack to the operator only.** Rejected for night work: the operator asked for round-the-clock progress, and an alert on a phone at night is not a response.
- **The watchdog wakes sessions by itself.** Rejected in the liveness design and by Codex: it needs every session's message token stored on disk, a standing secret outside the app that issued it.
- **A deputy with the Chief's full authority.** Rejected: it would duplicate the operator's delegation without the operator's word, and remove the independent review of the Chief's deliverables.
- **A bounded deputy with a recorded stand-in (chosen).**

## Consequences

- The Architect opens the watcher session only after items 9.1 to 9.5 hold.
- The DevHub controller builds the liveness read and the stand-in tag under the liveness design's own approvals.
- When this record is decided, the Architect lands the charter text and the status flip in one bc-docs pull request with a Codex docs landing, and both briefs are updated to cite this record.
- The record moves to `implemented` when the watcher has run under a recorded stand-in procedure and one handback has been acknowledged.

## Non-goals

No change to DEC-41e4bc's ten points, the roster, the responsibility map, the review ledger or the close gates. No authority for any host change. No deputy for any other controller.
