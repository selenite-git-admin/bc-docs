# Retire the external metric-audit exchange; metric audit collapses into bc-core as self-certification

## Context

The exchange never earned its keep, and this is the second time the same mistake was made. All 34 audit decisions ever recorded are PASS — the apparatus produced zero corrective signal on any metric. Every defect it caught in three weeks was a defect in itself: feed-name mismatch, envelope delivery, missing SELECT grants, a pickup EXECUTE regression, a supersession sequence collision. The transport carried only 3 decision events in its entire life, while the 34 audits that exist came through an older and simpler path — so an unproven mechanism had been made the mandatory gate for a path that demonstrably worked, blocking 126 packet-ready metrics.

This repeats D535 (DEC-b7d74b), where the courier was retired for exactly this property: it never stopped a wrong metric. Building a second courier and mistaking it for the cargo is the pattern this decision closes.

The driver was confirmed by the operator to be ASPIRATIONAL — no customer, certification body or investor commitment requires independent metric audit today. Independence therefore has zero external value at present while costing the roadmap: three weeks with zero metrics audited, against a metric-creation stack built in under a week.

Collapsing the boundary deletes rather than migrates the cost, because outbox, publication, pickup, envelopes, signing, sequences, continuity guards and the retirement/reissue lanes exist ONLY to cross a trust boundary. What carries the value — the cross-family maker/checker/moderator panel, the decision record, the evidence and certification — is already in bc-core, and metric_audit.* already lives in bc_platform_dev.

D483 (DEC-ffee4e) is the precedent: bc-ai was retired and its panels moved in-process to bc-core with the value preserved and only the service boundary deleted. This decision applies the same move to the audit exchange.

Standing rule adopted with this decision: no governance mechanism gets built unless a real defect it would have caught can be named, and the question "is this externally required or aspirational?" is asked before construction, not after three weeks.

## Decision

The external metric-audit exchange (auditor-side outbox/publication/pickup in bc_audit_dev, signed envelopes, feed sequences and continuity, retirement and reissue lanes, feed registrations) is RETIRED. Metric audit runs in-process in bc-core: panel -> decision -> evidence -> certification, with no envelope, no feed, and no cross-process transport.

RELABELLING IS MANDATORY AND IS THE SAFETY-CRITICAL PART OF THIS DECISION. bc-core writing its own verdicts is SELF-CERTIFICATION, not external audit. The phrase "externally audited" must not describe any state bc-core can reach on its own. In particular the D523 framing "active means externally audited and calculator-grade" is now false and is corrected to "active means panel-certified and calculator-grade". No document, ADR, deck, website, sales material or product surface may describe platform self-certification as external or independent audit.

RETIREMENT IS ADDITIVE, NOT DESTRUCTIVE. Nothing is deleted and no database change is made. The 34 existing decisions and their feed events remain exactly as they are — they are immutable history (Invariant III) and rewriting them would be the real hazard. The ~28 bc-core files implementing the exchange stay in place, marked deprecated and unused. bc-external-audit-ramp5 is archived by its owner, not by the platform. enforcement-3 remains permanently at checkpoint 1 as the durable record that the chain was abandoned there; the reissue packet 420a4816 is never consumed.

THE BRIDGE TO REAL EXTERNAL AUDIT IS NOT BURNED. The durable artifact was never the feed; it is the canonical decision record, which already carries decision_payload_digest, decision_digest and closure_root. If a customer or certification body ever requires independence, canonical decisions are exported and counter-signed by a genuine third party. A live signed feed is not needed for that.

SCOPE NOTE: "external auditor" in the compliance chapters means ISO 27001 / SOC 2 certification auditors. That is a different and legitimate concept and is untouched by this decision.

## Relationship to prior decisions

Precision matters here, because "retire the audit" would be the wrong reading. This decision retires the
**transport**, not the audit.

**Superseded — DEC-29c80b (D523)** only. Its lifecycle model stands; its claim that `active` means
*externally audited* does not, and is restated as *panel-certified*.

**PRESERVED and reinforced — DEC-05815d (D536).** The semantic axis of a metric audit must be the output
of a recorded, in-process, multi-model panel act rather than a checker-stamped claim. That is exactly what
survives, and it is the part carrying the value. An earlier draft of this ADR listed D536 as superseded;
that was an authoring error, corrected before commit.

**PRESERVED and supporting — DEC-dc5d52 (D531).** Authority by derivation — platform-internal evidence as
the sole intrinsic authority class — argues *for* this decision rather than against it. Also wrongly
listed as superseded in the same draft, and corrected.

**Pattern precedent — DEC-b7d74b (D535).** The courier was retired because it never stopped a wrong
metric. The exchange retired here has the identical property. Two instances make it a reflex worth naming:
governance apparatus must be able to point at a real defect it caught in the thing it governs.

**Mechanism precedent — DEC-ffee4e (D483).** bc-ai retired, panels moved in-process, value preserved, only
the service boundary deleted. This is the same move applied to the audit exchange.
